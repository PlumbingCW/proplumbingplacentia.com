5-Stage Reverse Osmosis Water Filter in Placentia, CA

Reverse osmosis cleans drinking water at one faucet. It does not soften the whole house, so know which problem you are solving.

By the Placentia Pro Plumbing team·Published October 29, 2026·Updated October 29, 2026

A five-stage reverse osmosis system is a filter that sits under a kitchen sink and delivers purified water through a small dedicated faucet. Homeowners in Placentia ask about them because they want better tasting drinking water or more control over what is in it.

This guide explains how the five stages work, what the system does and does not treat, and how it differs from a water softener. It leans on your own utility's water report, since that is where the real numbers are.

What the five stages usually do

Designs vary by brand, but a typical five-stage system works like this.

  • ✓Stage one, sediment filter. Catches sand, rust flakes and silt so they do not clog later stages.
  • ✓Stage two, carbon block. Reduces chlorine taste and odor and some organic compounds.
  • ✓Stage three, a second carbon stage. Adds further polishing before the membrane.
  • ✓Stage four, the RO membrane. Pushes water through a semipermeable membrane that rejects dissolved solids.
  • ✓Stage five, post carbon filter. Polishes taste from the storage tank before it reaches the faucet.

What reverse osmosis does and does not do

A reverse osmosis system treats drinking and cooking water at one tap. It is not a whole-house system, so showers, laundry and the water heater still see the same water as before.

That is a key difference from a softener. A salt-based softener removes the minerals that cause scale in pipes and heaters. An RO unit protects the glass of water you drink. Some homes use both, with the softener upstream of the membrane to protect it from scale.

The system also sends some water to the drain while it works, so it needs a drain saddle connection. If you want hardness handled for the whole house, the water softener installation page is the better starting point.

How an RO installation goes

Installation is usually a single planned visit.

  1. 01

    Pick the location

    The unit fits in the cabinet under the sink, with room for a storage tank.

  2. 02

    Mount the faucet

    A small dedicated faucet is mounted on the sink deck or counter.

  3. 03

    Connect supply and drain

    A tee feeds cold water to the unit and a saddle connects the drain line.

  4. 04

    Flush and test

    The system is flushed, checked for leaks and the tank is filled before use.

Questions to ask before buying a system

Ask for a performance data sheet that shows which contaminants the membrane is certified to reduce by an independent body. A brand that cannot show certification leaves you guessing.

Also ask about replacement costs over time, because filters and the membrane need changing on a schedule. A cheap unit with expensive filters costs more than it looks.

If you are already planning a heater or softener visit, the plumbing installation page explains how a plumber bundles connected work into one appointment.

Cost and upkeep you should plan for

The purchase price of the system is only part of the cost. Pre-filters are usually replaced about once a year, and the membrane lasts longer but still wears out. Ask the seller for the replacement schedule and part prices in writing, and compare systems on the cost over several years.

Reverse osmosis also sends a share of the incoming water down the drain as concentrate while it makes purified water. The ratio differs between models, so ask for the manufacturer's stated figure if water use matters to you.

Water pressure matters too. Membranes work best within a pressure range stated by the maker, and a home with low pressure may need a booster pump. The plumber can measure your pressure before you buy, so the unit you choose performs as advertised and you are not guessing about it.

Habits that keep an RO system working

  • ✓Change filters on schedule. Clogged pre-filters shorten membrane life.
  • ✓Sanitize periodically. Follow the manufacturer's guidance for the storage tank and lines.
  • ✓Watch for drips. Under-sink fittings can loosen over time, so check the cabinet occasionally.
  • ✓Know the shutoff. The unit has its own supply valve. Learn where it is.
