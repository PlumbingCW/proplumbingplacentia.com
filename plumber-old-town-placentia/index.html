Old Town is the historic core of Placentia. The city's own revitalization plan places it in the southern and western part of town, north of a stretch of the BNSF railroad, and measures it at 33 acres and 147 parcels. The same plan says residential neighborhoods surround it to the west, east and north. That mix of homes, shops and restaurants shapes the plumbing questions that come from here.

This page covers what is different about plumbing in an older, mixed-use part of town, what to check before you call, and who to contact about permits and the street sewer. To talk to a plumber about an Old Town address, call (714) 750-8637.

Why Old Town plumbing is worth a careful first look

Old Town grew up around 1910, when the Santa Fe Railroad was routed through Placentia and a station was built. Bradford Avenue and Melrose Avenue are named for two of the men who laid out the main streets. Buildings from that era and the decades after are where the district's age shows.

Age alone does not tell anyone what is inside a wall. Older buildings are often repiped in sections, patched after a repair or added onto, so a single property can hold several generations of pipe. That is why the first job on any visit is to look at what is actually there.

A sewer camera inspection or a leak detection visit shows how the pipes are behaving before anyone talks about replacing them. Opening a wall to guess costs more than a camera or a listening test, and it can leave you with a repair you did not need.

Plumbing problems that matter in an older mixed-use district

These are general plumbing problems, not a claim about any one building. They are the ones that tend to matter most when homes, apartments and food businesses share a few blocks.

  • ✓Drains that clog again after a week or two. A repeat clog usually means something is left in the line. Drain cleaning clears it, and a camera shows why it keeps coming back.
  • ✓Floor drains and grease lines in shops and restaurants. Kitchens and prep areas put heavy loads on drains. Commercial plumbing covers floor drains, restroom fixtures and small-building water heaters.
  • ✓Water heaters tucked into tight closets. Older buildings often have little room around the tank. Access changes how a water heater replacement is planned, so describe the location when you call.
  • ✓Slow leaks behind finished walls. A stain, a musty smell or a higher water bill can point to a hidden leak. A leak detection visit narrows it down without demolition.
  • ✓A main line that backs up. If several fixtures gurgle or back up together, the problem is further down the line. Sewer line cleaning is the usual next step.

Placentia, CA local note

Permits, sewer and water for Old Town addresses

Plumbing work such as water heaters, water lines and gas outlets can need a city permit. The Building and Safety Division is at 401 Chapman Avenue and can be reached at (714) 993-8124. Repairs are made to the California Plumbing Code, Title 24, Part 5, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

The city runs about 84 miles of gravity sewer, and the system is 50 to 100 years old. If a camera shows a blockage in the city main under the street, Public Works at (714) 993-8131 is the office to contact. Ask them who is responsible for the lateral, because the city page does not say.

Two water suppliers serve Placentia, Golden State Water and Yorba Linda Water District. Your bill shows which one is yours. We have not confirmed which utility serves each Old Town street.
