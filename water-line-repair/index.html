Emergency water line repair in Placentia, CA covers the supply pipe that carries water from the meter to your home. When it leaks, you may see wet ground, hear water near the meter, or lose pressure inside. The 24/7 line at (714) 750-8637 dispatches a licensed plumber to locate and repair the break.

A water service line is a pressurized pipe buried in soil, so a leak can run for weeks before it surfaces. The first signs are often a high bill, low pressure or a meter that runs with the house off. Finding where it fails, and whether the repair is a section or a replacement, is the plumber's first job.

Active problem right now

A water line leak that needs a call now

A slow leak in a yard can wait for the next appointment. A broken line pouring water across the ground or into the house cannot. Water can undermine paving, flood a garage and run into a foundation.

Treat it as urgent when any of these apply:

  1. Water is surfacing in the yard, driveway or near the meter. A fast-growing wet area means a line under pressure has broken.
  2. You have lost most of your water pressure. A sudden drop, with no work on the street, can point to a line break.
  3. The meter runs with the house off and the main valve closed. That points to the line between the meter and the house.
  4. There is no water at all. A line can fail completely, or a valve can seize. Call and describe exactly what you see.

If you can safely reach the main valve at the house, close it. If water is coming from the meter side, call the dispatcher, who can tell you whether the utility needs to be contacted too.

Which part of the line is yours and which is the utility's

The water line has a utility side and a property side, and the boundary matters because it decides who fixes what. The meter is the usual reference point. Ownership rules vary by provider, so the best step is to ask yours.

Golden State Water serves much of Placentia at 1-800-999-4033, and Yorba Linda Water District serves about a third of the city, so check your bill for who reads your meter. The plumber will tell you where the break sits relative to the meter so you know whether to call the provider, the plumber, or both. Work on your side of the meter follows the California Plumbing Code (Title 24 Part 5), which is based on the UPC.

Low water pressure: when the cause is the line

  • ✓One fixture is weak. The problem is usually a clogged aerator or a failing valve at that fixture, not the service line.
  • ✓Hot water is weak and cold is fine. The water heater, its valves or the hot side lines are more likely, and water heater repair covers that.
  • ✓Everything is weak, all the time. That points toward the service line, a failing shutoff valve or a pressure regulator. Older galvanized lines that have narrowed inside may need repiping rather than another patch.
  • ✓Pressure dropped suddenly along with a high bill. A leak in the line is a strong possibility and should be tested promptly.
  • ✓Pressure is weak and noisy. Banging or hammering can mean a valve or regulator problem.

What not to do when the service line is leaking

  • ✓Do not keep digging. Hand digging near a pressurized line can break it further or hit another buried utility.
  • ✓Do not run irrigation to test it. Extra water in a saturated yard hides the real damp spot.
  • ✓Do not touch the utility side of the meter. Shut the home valve only, and call the plumber or your water provider about the rest.
  • ✓Do not ignore a sudden pressure drop. Photograph the wet area and the meter so the plumber has something to compare.
