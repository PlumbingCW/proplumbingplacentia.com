What an installation includes beyond the heater itself

A new unit needs more than a place to sit. It needs a shutoff valve on the cold supply, a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe, flexible connectors, a drain, and the correct venting or electrical supply.

Many installations also add an expansion tank, which absorbs pressure when water heats and expands in a closed system. The plumber checks whether your supply pressure and plumbing call for one.

Earthquake straps close the list. California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped, and the installer does it as part of the job.

Choosing the fuel, size and location

Three choices drive the project, and each affects the others.

  • ✓Fuel. Gas, electric and heat pump units each need different hookups. Switching fuel means new gas lines, a new circuit or both.
  • ✓Size. Tank capacity should match the number of people and fixtures drawing hot water at the same time, not just the square footage.
  • ✓Location. Garage, closet, side yard and attic placements have different venting, clearance and drain needs. Moving a unit adds pipe runs.
  • ✓Venting. A gas unit needs a safe flue path. Some owners want to reroute a vent from a chimney to a side wall, which is a venting job with code rules of its own.
