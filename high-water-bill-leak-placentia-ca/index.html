High Water Bill in Placentia, CA? Check for a Hidden Leak

A jump in your water bill with no change in habits often points to a leak you cannot see. A ten minute meter check can tell you.

By the Placentia Pro Plumbing team·Published October 29, 2026·Updated October 29, 2026

If your water bill climbed and nothing in the household changed, a hidden leak is one of the first things to rule out. A running toilet, a slow pipe leak or a dripping irrigation line can waste a lot of water without leaving a visible puddle. The good news is that a simple meter check can tell you whether water is moving when nothing is on.

Start with the steps below. If the meter shows flow with everything off, or if you find damp spots, call (714) 750-8637 and a licensed plumber can be sent to find the source.

A ten minute meter test

This test needs no tools beyond a flashlight. Do it when no one is using water.

  1. 01

    Turn off everything

    Make sure no taps, appliances, irrigation or ice makers are running. Check that no one flushes during the test.

  2. 02

    Find the water meter

    It is usually in a box near the curb or the front of the property. Lift the lid carefully and check for insects or debris first.

  3. 03

    Read the dial or leak indicator

    Many meters have a small triangle or dial that spins when any water is moving. If it turns with everything off, water is flowing somewhere.

  4. 04

    Wait and recheck

    Wait 10 to 15 minutes without using water, then read again. A change on the dial confirms a leak on your side of the meter.

The usual places a hidden leak hides

Once the meter test says water is moving, these are the common sources to look at.

  • ✓A running toilet. A flapper that does not seal lets water trickle from the tank to the bowl. Add a drop of food coloring to the tank. If color shows in the bowl without flushing, the flapper leaks.
  • ✓A leaking faucet or showerhead. Slow drips add up, especially on the hot side, so check them all.
  • ✓Irrigation lines and sprinklers. A broken head or a cracked line can leak under the lawn for weeks without obvious signs.
  • ✓Pipes in the wall or under the floor. Pinholes, failed joints and corroded sections can leak inside a wall or beneath a slab.
  • ✓The water heater and its connections. Check the base and the supply lines for moisture or rust.

Who to call about a Placentia water bill

Golden State Water lists a Placentia service area, and the Yorba Linda Water District serves roughly one third of Placentia, so check your bill to see which utility you have. Golden State Water's customer line is 1-800-999-4033. Ask your utility about the meter reading, your usage history, and whether it offers a leak alert. Once a leak is found, the repair is made to the California Plumbing Code, Title 24, Part 5, which follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC).

Do not rely on a bill figure alone. Rates, billing cycles and seasonal use all change the total, so compare the usage numbers rather than the dollars. What to do when a suspected leak turns urgent

Most high bill leaks are slow. A few signs mean water is escaping fast or causing damage.

Call the 24/7 line at (714) 750-8637 if you notice any of these:

  1. The meter runs fast with everything off. A fast spin means a significant leak.
  2. Water appears at the foundation, in a wall, or on a floor. The leak may be damaging the structure.
  3. The floor feels warm or you hear running water in a quiet house. That can point to a hot line under the slab.
  4. Water pressure suddenly drops. A major break can reduce pressure at every tap.

Shut off the main valve while you wait. If you cannot find it, the dispatcher can walk you through the options. A slab leak repair may be needed if the leak is under the floor.

What a leak detection visit looks like

A plumber starts with the meter and pressure checks, then narrows the search with listening equipment and thermal tools that find water without opening walls first. Once the leak is located, you get a clear price before work starts.

Pinpointing the source first limits the opening needed and the repair cost. It also confirms whether one leak or several are behind the bill.
