Call now if water is moving where it should not

An emergency plumber in Placentia, CA is the person you call when waiting until morning costs you more than calling tonight. Water on a ceiling, a pipe spraying inside a wall or sewage rising in a tub all fit that description. The line at (714) 750-8637 is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays.

While you dial, do these things in this order:

  1. Shut off the water. The main shutoff is usually where the supply line enters the house, often near the front hose bib or the garage wall. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Stop using fixtures. Do not flush, run the washer or open taps until the plumber says it is safe.
  3. Protect the electrics. If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, keep people away and switch off power only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely.
  4. Move what you can. Lift rugs, electronics and paperwork out of the wet area. Photograph the damage for your records.

A gas smell is different. Leave the house first and then call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 or 911 if there is danger. Plumbing questions can wait until everyone is outside.

If the fix is bigger than a quick repair, you get the options and the choice stays yours.

Why Placentia plumbing has its own pattern of problems

Placentia was incorporated in December 1926, and its population jumped from about 5,000 in 1960 to nearly 25,000 by 1970. California plumbing work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Placentia's growth means a large share of homes are now 50 years old or older, and old supply lines, shutoff valves and drain pipes are the ones that fail without much warning.

The oldest parts of town, including Old Town near the Santa Fe railroad corridor and the Atwood area, hold some of the oldest plumbing. If a house there still has its original pipes, a leak under the slab or a corroded valve is a reasonable thing to expect, and a camera or leak survey is cheaper than guessing.

Water service is also split. Golden State Water covers much of Placentia, and Yorba Linda Water District serves about one third of the city, so check your bill before you call a utility. Golden State Water reports average hardness of about 11 grains per gallon, which leaves scale inside water heaters and fixtures over time. A water heater repair call often ends with a flush that scale made necessary.

The city's own sewer system is roughly 50 to 100 years old with about 84 miles of gravity pipe. When a main line backs up, a camera shows whether the problem is your lateral or the city main. That is the question that decides who fixes it, and the sewer line repair page explains the difference.

Problems that count as an emergency in Placentia homes

Not every plumbing problem needs a midnight call. These do, because waiting makes the damage or the health risk worse.

  • ✓A burst or sprayed pipe. Water that cannot be stopped at the fixture needs the main shutoff and a plumber, see burst pipe repair.
  • ✓Sewage coming up. Water rising in a tub or floor drain, or sewage smell indoors, means the main line is blocked. Emergency drain cleaning is the first step.
  • ✓A water heater leaking at the base. A tank that is wet underneath is usually past repair, and water heater replacement is the next call.
  • ✓A hidden leak you can hear or measure. A hissing wall, a warm patch on the floor or a meter that keeps turning with everything off points to slab leak repair or a line leak.
  • ✓No water at all. A failed supply line or valve can leave a house dry, and water line repair restores it.
