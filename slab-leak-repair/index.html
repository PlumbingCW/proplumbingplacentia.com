A slab leak is a leak in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete foundation. Because the pipe is buried, the first sign is often indirect: a warm patch on the floor, the sound of running water, a damp baseboard or a bill that jumps. The repair sequence is locate, decide, fix, restore.

Active problem right now

Signs that water is coming up through the floor right now

A slab leak is under pressure, so it does not pause overnight. Water escapes into the soil and can surface at flooring edges, door thresholds and baseboards. Time matters because wet subfloor and trim swell quickly.

Call at once if you notice any of these:

  1. A floor spot feels warm. A leak on a hot water line under the slab can warm the concrete above it. Bare feet find this before eyes do.
  2. You hear water running with every tap closed. A hissing or rushing sound from the floor or a wall means a pressurized line is open.
  3. Water appears at the baseboard or under flooring. Laminate that lifts, tile that cracks and carpet that squelches all point downward.
  4. The meter runs with the whole house off. That is the clearest measurable sign, and it is described step by step on the leak detection page.

Close the main valve, move rugs and furniture off the wet area, and call. Do not run the water heater if the hot side is the suspect, because it can keep feeding the leak.

Why pipes under a slab fail

Lines under a slab sit in contact with soil and concrete for decades. Over time they can corrode from the outside, rub against rough rock or concrete edges as the house settles slightly, or fail at a fitting that was fine when installed. Hot water lines tend to work harder because they expand and contract each day.

Water chemistry also plays a role. Golden State Water reports an average hardness near 11 grains per gallon in its Placentia-Yorba Linda system, which is on the hard side, and scale can narrow and stress pipe over a long period. That does not mean your pipe is failing, but it is one reason to have a plumber look when symptoms start. Any new section is run to the UPC, the model code behind California's Title 24 Part 5 plumbing code.

From the first warm spot to a dry floor

A slab leak repair follows a clear sequence, and each step is explained before it happens.

  1. 01

    Confirm and isolate

    The plumber checks the meter, shuts off sections of line and pressure tests to prove the leak is under the slab and not inside a wall.

  2. 02

    Pinpoint from above

    Listening gear and thermal imaging mark the spot on the floor, so any opening is small and in the right place.

  3. 03

    Choose the repair route

    You are shown the options, which can include breaking the slab at the spot, rerouting the line through walls and attic, or replacing the section.

  4. 04

    Repair and pressure test

    The line is repaired, tested and documented. Restoration of flooring and concrete is discussed so the scope is clear.

Three ways to fix a leak under the concrete

The best route depends on where the leak is, how many lines are failing and how the home is laid out. Ask the plumber to explain why one fits your case.

Spot repair through the slabConcrete is opened directly over the leak and the pipe section is replacedFast for a single leak, but flooring over the spot is disturbed

Slab leaks that start somewhere else

  • ✓The water heater area. Callers often describe water at the edge of the slab near the garage or heater closet. A tank, a connector or the line feeding it can be the source, so the water heater repair page is worth a look.
  • ✓A drain, not a supply line. Damp but not warm floors with no meter movement can mean a drain or shower pan leak. A pipe camera sorts that out.
  • ✓Outdoor or driveway slabs. A line under a driveway or patio slab is found the same way, and cutting there is simpler than under finished flooring.
  • ✓A leak in the wall above. Water can run down a wall cavity and pool at the slab edge, which looks like a slab leak but is not.

What to do while you wait for the plumber

Close the main valve and leave it closed until the plumber says otherwise. Switch off the water heater at its control or breaker if the hot side is suspected, and move rugs, boxes and furniture off the wet floor so the flooring can start to dry.

Write down what you noticed and when, including the meter reading if you can see it. A short timeline helps the plumber narrow the search and gives your insurer a clear record. Do not drill or cut into the floor yourself, because a mistaken hole can hit another line or a cable and makes the finding harder.

If the floor is wet near a wall outlet, an appliance or the panel, stay out of the room. Water and electricity do not mix, and the plumber can make the area safe once on site.

What changes the price of a slab leak repair

A clear price is given before work starts. These are the factors that decide the number.

Location of the leak

A leak near an exterior wall or a garage slab is easier to reach than one under a kitchen or a main hallway.

Repair route

Spot repair, rerouting and replacement carry different labor and materials.

Number of leaks

One leak is a repair. Several leaks suggest the line itself is failing.

Flooring above

Tile, hardwood and carpet each change the restoration scope.

Access and cleanup

Furniture removal, protection of finished spaces and debris haul-off add time.

Home policies often cover resulting water damage but may not cover the cost of accessing the leak under the slab, and terms vary. Ask your agent before work begins and keep the plumber's finding, photos and invoice together.
