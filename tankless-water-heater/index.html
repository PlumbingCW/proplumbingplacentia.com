A tankless unit heats water as it flows instead of storing it. That can save space and give a steady supply, but it needs the right fuel capacity, venting and regular upkeep. Hard water makes the upkeep part more important than many owners expect.

Why tankless units need a flush in Placentia

Inside a tankless heater, water passes through a heat exchanger with narrow passages. Minerals in the water bake onto the surfaces and narrow those passages, which cuts flow, raises the temperature the unit must work at and often triggers an error code.

Golden State Water's 2026 report for the Placentia and Yorba Linda system lists hardness averaging 11.0 grains per gallon, with a range from 3.92 to 17.5. That is enough for scale to build up over time. Check your bill, because Yorba Linda Water District serves about one third of the city and its water may differ.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the exchanger, then rinses it out. It is the single most useful maintenance step for a tankless unit, and many owners schedule it on a regular interval.

Signs a tankless unit needs service

  • ✓Water that swings hot and cold. Scale in the exchanger or a sensor fault can cause temperature swings.
  • ✓An error code on the display. Note the code and the brand when you call. Many codes point to flow, ignition or venting issues.
  • ✓Reduced flow at the tap. Clogged inlet screens or scale narrowing the exchanger reduce flow.
  • ✓A leak at or under the unit. Water at the base can be a failed fitting or a failed exchanger. The plumber traces it.
  • ✓A unit that was never flushed. If you inherited the home and no one remembers a service, it is overdue.

Installing or switching to tankless

A switch to tankless is a bigger project than a tank swap. Gas models often need a larger gas line than a tank unit, a dedicated venting path and sometimes a condensate drain. Electric models can need a large circuit and panel capacity.

The plumber measures the existing hookups, the run to the new location and the fixtures drawing hot water, then explains what the unit needs. If an existing tank is failing at the same time, the decision may begin on the water heater repair page.

Earthquake bracing rules under California Health and Safety Code section 19211 apply to water heaters broadly, and the plumber confirms how they apply to a wall-mounted unit. Ask the Building & Safety Division at (714) 993-8124 about permit questions. The California Plumbing Code, Title 24 Part 5, is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), so ask your installer how it applies to a wall-mounted unit.

Questions to settle before choosing a unit

These keep a tankless project from running into surprises.

Gas line capacity

Can the existing line carry the unit's demand, or does it need a larger run?

Venting path

Where does the flue exit, and can the wall or roof route be used?

Electric supply

Does the panel have room for the circuit an electric model asks for?

Water quality

Will scale be a recurring issue? A water softener can reduce it.

Safety steps for gas and electric tankless units

  • ✓Smell gas, leave first. Get everyone outside and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200, then 911 if there is danger.
  • ✓Do not reset a unit that keeps faulting. Repeated error codes mean a cause needs diagnosing.
  • ✓Keep the vent path clear. Do not store items against the exhaust or air intake.
  • ✓Shut off power and water before a flush. Turn off the breaker or gas valve first, then close the isolation valves.
