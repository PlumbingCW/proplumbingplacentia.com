Bathtub Faucet Leak Repair in Placentia, CA

A dripping tub faucet wastes water and stains the porcelain. The fix is usually a small part, but finding the right one takes a little care.

By the Placentia Pro Plumbing team·Published October 29, 2026·Updated October 29, 2026

A bathtub faucet that drips after you turn it off is almost always a worn internal part: a cartridge, a washer, a seat or an O-ring. The drip itself is small, but it adds up on a water bill, and it often gets worse over time. If the leak is heavy or you cannot shut it off, call (714) 750-8637 and a licensed plumber will be sent.

This guide covers the common causes, what to check before you touch a tool, and how hard water in the Placentia area plays a part. It also explains the difference between a faucet drip and a leak inside the wall.

Why a bathtub faucet leaks

Tub faucets come in several designs, but the causes of a leak fall into a short list.

  • ✓A worn cartridge. Single-handle and many two-handle faucets use a cartridge that seals the flow. Over time the seals wear and let water pass.
  • ✓Old washers and seats. Compression faucets press a rubber washer onto a metal seat. Washers harden and seats pit, so the seal fails.
  • ✓Failed O-rings. Small rubber rings around the stem or spout dry out, which causes weeping at the handle or base.
  • ✓Mineral scale. Hard water leaves deposits that roughen the sealing surfaces and keep parts from closing fully.
  • ✓A diverter that no longer seals. If water drips from the spout while you shower, the diverter valve may be letting water bypass.

What to do before you call

A few simple checks help narrow down the cause. None need special tools.

  1. 01

    Find where the water comes from

    Is it the spout, the handle, or the wall behind the trim? A spout drip points to the cartridge or seat. A handle drip points to O-rings or the stem.

  2. 02

    Try the other handle

    On two-handle faucets, close each handle fully. If the drip stops when you tighten the hot side only, that side is the culprit.

  3. 03

    Look for wall stains

    A stain or soft area around or below the tub could mean a leak inside the wall rather than at the faucet.

  4. 04

    Shut off the water to the tub if you can

    Some homes have local shutoffs. If not, you may need to close the main valve, so know where it is.

Hard water and tub faucet wear

Mineral scale is one of the quieter causes of faucet failure. When water evaporates around a stem or seat, it leaves calcium and magnesium behind. Over months and years that buildup roughens parts that are meant to be smooth and keeps a cartridge from seating fully.

If you notice white crust on the tub spout or around the handle, scale is part of the story. A plumber replacing the cartridge can also clean the seat, which often makes the new part last longer. When a tub faucet leak is an emergency

Most faucet drips can wait until morning. A few cannot.

Call the 24/7 line at (714) 750-8637 if any of these apply:

  1. Water will not shut off at all. A faucet that runs freely can overflow a tub or run behind a wall.
  2. You see water coming through a ceiling below the bathroom. The leak may be in the wall or the supply line.
  3. The handle spins freely or comes off. The stem may have failed and water can spray out.
  4. There is a wet wall or soft floor near the tub. Moisture trapped behind the surround can damage framing and grow mold.

Shut off the water at the nearest valve or the main while you wait, and keep power off to any wet outlets nearby.
