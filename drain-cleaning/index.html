Drain cleaning is the work of removing whatever narrows a drain line, then proving the line runs freely afterward. The usual culprits are grease, hair, soap scum, wipes, paper and tree roots. A good visit ends with the cause named, not just the water gone.

When a clogged drain cannot wait until morning

One slow sink is a nuisance. Water rising in a tub when you flush, or sewage smell from a floor drain, is a different problem. That pattern points to the main line, and every minute of use pushes more wastewater back into the house.

Treat it as an emergency when any of these show up:

  1. Several fixtures are affected at once. A toilet that gurgles while the shower drains slowly usually shares one blocked line.
  2. Water is coming up, not going down. A tub, shower or floor drain filling when you run the washer is a backup in progress.
  3. You smell sewage inside. Open a window, keep children and pets away from the wet floor, and stop using water in the house.
  4. The overflow is near outlets or appliances. Keep people out of the area and shut off power to that room if the breaker panel is dry and safe to reach.

If the toilet is overflowing right now, close the supply valve on the wall behind it. That stops new water but does not touch the blockage, so call before you try anything more.

One fixture or the whole house: how the plumber tells which line is blocked

Every fixture has its own short branch line, and all of those branches feed a main drain that runs out to the sewer connection. A clog in a branch affects one sink or tub. A clog in the main affects everything below it, usually the lowest fixtures first, which is why a ground-floor shower or floor drain is often the first place a backup shows.

The quick test is simple. Run water at a different sink, then flush a toilet that is far from the problem fixture. If those drain normally, the blockage is local. If a second fixture gurgles or rises, the plumber will head for the cleanout, the capped access pipe that gives a cable direct entry to the main line.

Knowing which line is blocked matters for cost and for the method. A branch is often cleared with a hand-fed or small machine cable. A main line may need a heavier cable or hydro jetting, and a repeat clog calls for a look inside with a sewer camera.

Kitchen sink clogs: grease, disposals and the dishwasher line

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains because the buildup is fat, not hair. Hot grease runs down as a liquid, cools inside a pipe that is often only 1.5 to 2 inches wide, and hardens into a ring that narrows a little more with every meal.

  • ✓Grease and food film. The pipe wall slowly lines with fat and starch. Water still drains, just slower each month, until a piece of rice or an eggshell closes the gap.
  • ✓A jammed or undersized disposal. Food that gets past the grinder lands in the trap and branch. A garbage disposal repair visit often goes hand in hand with a cable run on the same line.
  • ✓The dishwasher hose. The dishwasher drains through the same branch. If dirty water backs up into the sink when the dishwasher runs, the shared line is partly blocked.
  • ✓A double sink that drains unevenly. One basin clearing and the other holding water points to a clog past the tee, not at the strainer.

Cable, jet or camera: how the method gets chosen

The right tool depends on what is blocking the pipe and how far away it sits. Plumbers do not start with the biggest machine, because a heavy tool in a fragile old line can do harm.

MethodBest forLimits
Hand or drum cable (snaking)Hair, paper and soft clogs in sinks, tubs and short branchesPokes a hole through the blockage but can leave grease on the pipe wall
Larger machine cable with cutting headsRoots and stubborn buildup in main lines of 3 to 4 inchesNeeds a cleanout or a pulled toilet for access
Hydro jettingGrease, scale and silt scoured off the full pipe wallNot suited to a collapsed or broken line, which the camera should rule out first
Camera inspectionRepeat clogs, unknown causes, and proof the line is clearShows the problem but does not clear it on its own

Hydro jetting pressure on home jets commonly runs from about 1,500 to 4,000 PSI. Ask the plumber which setting is used on older pipe.

What drives the price of clearing a drain

A clear price is given before work starts, and these are the factors that move it.

Where the blockage sits

A fixture trap is quick. A main line behind a cleanout takes more time and heavier equipment.

Access

A real cleanout speeds everything up. With none, the plumber may need to pull a toilet or work from a roof vent.

Method required

A basic cable run costs less than jetting, and a camera inspection is its own line item.

Root or scale severity

Heavy roots need cutting heads and sometimes a follow-up visit. Hard water scale slows the work.

Time of the call

After-hours dispatch carries a different rate than a scheduled daytime visit.

Insurance rarely pays for the cleaning itself, because a clog is a maintenance problem. A sewer backup that damages floors is a separate coverage item on many policies, so read yours or ask your agent before you throw out wet flooring.

Keeping drains open after the visit

  • ✓Wipe pans before washing. A paper towel removes most grease before it reaches the pipe.
  • ✓Flush toilet paper only. Wipes sold as flushable still tangle in pipe joints and roots.
  • ✓Catch hair at the tub. A cheap strainer costs less than one service call, and you empty it by hand.
  • ✓Skip chemical openers. They can sit in a standing pipe and harm fittings and the person who works on it next. The bleach question is covered in a homeowner guide on this site.
