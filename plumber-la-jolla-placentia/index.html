Shared drains in apartment buildings

In a building with several units, drains often join into one shared line. When water backs up in the lowest unit, the cause can sit upstream, in another unit or in the building's main line. That makes the symptom a poor guide to where the blockage is.

A drain cleaning visit clears the line, and hydro jetting scours it when grease or buildup keeps coming back. A sewer camera inspection shows the real cause, which is more useful to an owner than clearing the same clog every few weeks.

Signs it is time to call

Waiting rarely makes these better.

  • !More than one fixture draining slowly. That points to the shared or main line rather than a single sink.
  • !Water stains on a ceiling or wall. A leak can run for weeks before it shows, see leak detection.
  • !A water bill that jumps with no change in use. A hidden leak is a common cause.
  • !No hot water, or a tank that is wet underneath. See water heater repair and replacement.
  • !A sewage smell indoors. Stop using fixtures on that line and call.

If you rent: before you call

A few steps keep a rental repair from stalling.

Tell the property manager

Most leases ask you to report plumbing problems promptly, so do it in writing if you can.

Close the nearest valve

If water is running, close the valve under the fixture or the main shutoff if you know where it is.

Photograph the damage

Pictures help the owner and any insurer see what happened.

Ask who approves repairs

The owner may need to approve a plumber before work starts.

Older single-family homes

Neighborhood descriptions call many La Jolla houses older, and older houses can have a mix of original and replaced pipes. We do not assume what is behind a wall from the age of a home. A visit starts by looking at the symptom and the fixtures.

If repeated leaks point to failing supply lines, repiping is something to price and compare, not something to rush. For a tank that is past its useful life, water heater replacement is usually the cleaner choice than repeated repairs.
