When a tank has failed and the water is already out

A tank that leaks from the shell cannot be patched. Water will keep escaping, and the longer it runs the more it spreads under walls and flooring.

Do these things now:

  1. Close the cold supply valve above the heater and open a hot tap in the house to take pressure off the tank.
  2. Cut the fuel or power. Breaker off for electric. Gas control to off for gas.
  3. Stay clear of standing water near outlets. If the water reaches a panel or appliance, keep out of the room.
  4. Photograph everything. Take pictures before you clean, in case a claim follows.

A gas smell means leave and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from a safe place. Call 911 if there is danger.

How to tell a tank is finished

Water around the base is the plainest sign, but it is not the only one. Rust streaks on the tank, rusty hot water, rumbling that returns right after a flush and a pan that keeps filling all point the same way.

Age matters too. The serial number on the data label encodes the build date, and the plumber can read it for you. A unit well past the middle of its expected life that needs a repair is often better replaced than patched.

Many owners also decide to replace before a failure. A planned swap on your schedule costs less in damage than a midnight flood.

Heat pump, gas or electric: what changes the choice

Most replacements stay with the fuel already in the house, because the gas line, flue or circuit is already there. That keeps the job short and the price easier to predict.

A move to a heat pump water heater is a different kind of project. These units need electric supply, clearance for airflow and a place where cooler exhaust air is acceptable, such as a garage. Owners who ask about them usually want lower running cost, and the plumber can explain what your space allows.

Whatever you choose, ask how the new unit will be vented or powered, where the drain goes, and how the old tank leaves the house. Those three answers show whether the quote is complete.

Bracing, permits and the rules that apply in Placentia

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake movement. A replacement unit has to meet it, and the installer handles the straps as part of the job.

The City of Placentia's plumbing permit form lists water heaters among its items. Rules and fees are set by the Building & Safety Division, so ask them at (714) 993-8124 what applies to your swap.

The California Plumbing Code, Title 24 Part 5, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code, governs relief valves, discharge piping and venting. Licensed plumbers work to it, and you can verify a license on the Contractors State License Board site. That code descends from the UPC, so a licensed plumber can point to the rule behind a given requirement.

Hard water and a longer life for the new tank

  • ✓Know your water. Golden State Water reported hardness averaging 11.0 grains per gallon in its 2026 report, with a range from 3.92 to 17.5. Your own bill shows which utility serves you, and Yorba Linda Water District covers about a third of the city.
  • ✓Flush on a schedule. Draining a few gallons from the tank each year moves loose sediment out before it hardens on the bottom.
  • ✓Check the anode rod. This sacrificial rod protects the tank from corrosion and wears out first. Ask the plumber to look at it on service visits.
  • ✓Consider a softener. If scale is already a problem on your fixtures, see the water softener installation page.

What a replacement quote should cover

Ask for these items by name so the price you hear is the price you pay.

The unit itself

Brand, size, fuel type and recovery rate for the tank you are being quoted.

Connections and parts

New flex connectors, shutoff valve, relief valve and discharge pipe if the old ones are worn.

Venting or electrical work

Any change to a flue, a gas line or a circuit, with the reason stated.

Haul-away and cleanup

Removal of the old tank and the water around it.

Why replacement prices differ from house to house

A clear price is given before work starts. These are the factors that move it.

Tank size and type

A larger tank or a heat pump unit costs more than a standard tank.

Fuel changes

Switching between gas and electric means new lines, a new circuit or venting work.

Code upgrades

Old shutoff valves, missing bracing and worn discharge pipes are brought up to current practice.

Location and access

A cramped closet or an attic platform adds labor.

Timing

Same-day and after-hours calls are priced differently from a booked visit.
