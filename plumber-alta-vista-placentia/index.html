Working with an HOA or a gated entrance

If your home is in an association, a few steps up front keep a plumbing job from stalling.

  1. 01

    Read your HOA rules

    Check whether work on exterior or shared plumbing needs approval before it starts.

  2. 02

    Tell the gate or management office

    Give them the plumber's visit window so the crew is let in without delay.

  3. 03

    Know your shutoffs

    Locate the main water shutoff for your home and ask whether any shared lines serve your building. For a gas smell, leave the home and call SoCalGas at 1-800-427-2200 from outside.

  4. 04

    Give your community and street

    Say Alta Vista, name the street and mention any gate code process when you call.

Attached and detached homes in one community

Listing sites describe both attached and single-family homes in parts of Alta Vista. In an attached home, walls and sometimes drain lines are shared with a neighbor, so a leak can travel further than it would in a detached house.

Ask your association what its master insurance covers and what it expects you to handle. That answer decides whether you call a plumber, the HOA or both, and it is worth knowing before a leak happens. A leak detection visit finds the source so the right party can be contacted.

Water hardness and your water heater

Golden State Water reports average hardness of about 188 mg/L, or 11.0 grains per gallon, for its Placentia and Yorba Linda system. That is well into the range where scale shows up on fixtures and builds inside water heaters.

Which utility serves your street is a question for your bill, because Yorba Linda Water District also serves part of Placentia and we have not confirmed which one serves each part of Alta Vista. If your supply is hard, flushing a tank, adding a softener or choosing a tankless unit with scale in mind can all help.
