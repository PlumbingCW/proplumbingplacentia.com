Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer line that has failed lets wastewater back up into the lowest fixtures in the house. Floor drains, tubs and ground-floor toilets are usually first. This is a health risk as well as a damage risk.

Take these steps right away:

  1. Stop using water. Every drop that goes down adds to the backup.
  2. Keep children and pets out. Sewage carries germs, so treat the wet area as contaminated.
  3. Shut off power to a wet room only if the panel is dry. Never walk through standing water to reach a panel.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see. Mention gurgling, smell, and which fixtures are affected.

Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line. It can sit in the pipe and harm the plumber who works on it next.

Signs the pipe itself has failed

A blocked line clears with a cable or a jet. A failed line does not stay clear. Watch for patterns that point to damage in the pipe.

Repeated clogs in the same place suggest a sag, an offset joint or roots. Gurgling toilets, a sewage smell in the yard, soft or sunken ground along the line and slow drains in every fixture all point the same way. A sewer camera inspection shows what is happening inside.

Cast iron pipe in older Placentia homes

Older homes can still have cast iron drain pipe inside the walls or under the slab. Cast iron rusts from the inside and from outside moisture, it scales, and the rough interior catches debris. Cracks and corroded joints are common as it ages.

Cast iron repair often uses a repair sleeve or coupling to bridge a crack, or a section is cut out and replaced with modern pipe. A badly corroded stack may need a longer replacement. The camera shows how far the damage goes.

Roots, offsets and bellies: what each means for the repair

The camera footage usually shows one of a few problems, and each leads to a different repair plan.

  • •Roots at a joint. Roots find the small gap where two pipe sections meet. Once they are cut out, the joint still needs sealing, or the roots return. Regular hydro jetting only buys time if the gap stays open.
  • •An offset joint. Soil movement can shift one pipe section against the next. The step catches paper and solids, and the clog keeps returning at the same spot.
  • •A belly. A section that has settled holds water and solids in a low spot. Cleaning helps for a while, but the section usually has to be re-laid at the right slope.
  • •A crushed or collapsed pipe. A cable stops dead at the same distance and the camera cannot pass. This is the case where replacement, by trench or trenchless methods, is on the table.

How to compare repair quotes without guessing

Ask every plumber for the camera footage and the distance to the problem. A quote that does not point to a specific spot on the footage is a guess. If two plumbers describe different damage on the same line, compare the video, not the sales talk.

Ask what the repair covers and what it leaves alone. A spot repair fixes one section and the rest of the pipe stays as it was. A liner or a replacement changes the whole run. Neither is better in every case, and the footage decides which fits.

Ask whether the price includes a final camera run, restoration of any dug area and the permit if one is needed. For permit questions, the Building & Safety Division at (714) 993-8124 is the office to ask. Then compare the quotes on the same scope.
