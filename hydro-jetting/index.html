Sewer backup now: what to do before the jet truck arrives

A backup that reaches the lowest drain in the house means the main line is blocked downstream. Every flush and every load of laundry adds to the problem. The goal until help arrives is to stop adding water and protect the floor.

Work through this short list while you wait:

  1. Stop all water use. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers until the line is cleared.
  2. Keep people and pets out of the wet area. Backed-up water can carry bacteria, so wear rubber boots or gloves if you must go in.
  3. Move rugs and belongings above the water line. Lift what you can onto furniture or counters.
  4. Leave the breaker alone if the panel is wet or the floor is flooded. If the panel is dry and clear of water, switch off power to the affected room only.

Tell the dispatcher when the first fixture backed up and which one it was. That detail helps the plumber decide whether to start with a cable, a camera or the jet.

How a hydro jet actually cleans a pipe

A jet hose carries water to a nozzle with several small holes. Most holes point back along the pipe, which pulls the hose forward, and one or two point forward to break up the plug in front. As the hose is drawn back, the spray scours the whole inside wall in a ring.

That ring-shaped clean is the difference from a cable. A cable cuts a channel through the middle of a blockage and leaves the rest of the buildup on the wall. The line then narrows again, which is why a drain cleared by cable can clog again within weeks.

A good crew starts with a lower setting and works up only as the pipe allows. Water pressure and nozzle choice are matched to the pipe size and material, then the line is run with clean water at the end to show it flows.

Signs a cable is not enough

Some blockages come back no matter how many times they are cabled. These are the patterns that point toward jetting.

  • ✓A cable stalled partway down the line. If the cable stops at the same distance each time, there may be a heavy buildup, a root mass or a damaged joint at that point.
  • ✓The clog returns within weeks. Grease and scale left on the wall rebuild the blockage quickly. That calls for the whole wall to be scoured.
  • ✓Root hair keeps growing back. Fine roots enter at joints and soak up the moisture in the pipe. Jetting clears them, and a later repair deals with where they got in.
  • ✓Slow drains across the house. When every fixture drains slowly, the main is narrowed along its length, not blocked at one point.
  • ✓A restaurant or shop line with fat buildup. Commercial kitchens put grease into pipes all day. Jetting is a normal part of keeping those lines moving.

Jetting compared with cabling and a camera

The three tools do different jobs, and a good plumber often uses more than one on the same visit.

ToolWhat it does wellWhat it cannot do
Cable with cutting headBreaks through a root plug or hard obstructionLeaves buildup on the pipe wall
Hydro jetScours grease, scale, silt and root hair from the whole wallNot for a collapsed, offset or cracked pipe
CameraShows the cause, the distance and the pipe conditionDoes not clear the line by itself

If the camera shows a broken or collapsed section, jetting stops and the conversation turns to sewer line repair.

Old pipe, roots and why a camera comes first

Jet water is gentle on a healthy pipe and hard on a weak one. A cracked clay joint, a pipe that has sagged, or a section with heavy scale and thin walls can be damaged by the wrong pressure. That is why a sewer camera inspection before or right after jetting is good practice.

What a hydro jetting visit looks like

The sequence is the same for most homes, with small changes when the cleanout is hard to reach.

  1. 01

    Call and describe the backup

    Say which fixture backed up first, whether more than one is affected, and how long the line has been slow.

  2. 02

    Find the access point

    The plumber looks for the cleanout. If there is none, access is gained through a toilet flange or a roof vent.

  3. 03

    Camera, then jet

    The line is viewed first when possible, then jetted from the cleanout toward the street connection.

  4. 04

    Flow test and next steps

    Water is run to prove the line is moving. You hear what was found and whether a repair or a follow-up camera visit is advised.

Grease, restaurants and commercial lines

Commercial kitchens, salons and shops put the heaviest load on a drain. Jetting is the usual way to keep those lines open between pumping visits.

  • •Kitchen waste lines. Fat from fryers and dishwashers cools in the pipe and hardens. A jet removes the whole ring, not just a channel.
  • •Floor drains. Slow floor drains in back-of-house areas are often the first sign of a narrowing branch.
  • •Grease traps and interceptors. The trap itself is pumped on a schedule. The pipes between the trap and the sewer can still need jetting. See commercial plumbing for business service.
