Main line backing up: do this now

When the main is blocked, there is nowhere for wastewater to go. A small clog today becomes a flooded bathroom tomorrow.

Take these steps:

  1. Stop all water use. That includes laundry, dishwasher and showers.
  2. Do not use the toilet. Each flush adds to the backup.
  3. Find the cleanout if you can. A capped pipe in the yard or along the house wall helps the plumber start quickly.
  4. Keep the area dry and clear. Lift items off the floor, and avoid touching wet electrical items.

Tell the dispatcher which fixture backed up first. That helps the plumber decide where to start.

What a main line clog looks like

Several fixtures slow down together, with gurgling sounds in nearby drains. A toilet flush may bring water up in a tub. A sewage smell may come from a floor drain.

A clog in a single sink or toilet is a branch problem. A clog in the main affects everything below it. If you are not sure, run water at one sink and flush a distant toilet to see whether the problem spreads.

Common causes of main line blockages

Main lines clog for a handful of reasons, and the cause decides the method.

  • •Tree roots. Roots enter at joints and cracks and form a mat. Cutting heads remove them, and a repair stops them returning.
  • •Grease and scale. Buildup narrows the wall over time. Jetting scours it away. See hydro jetting.
  • •Wipes and paper products. Items marketed as flushable can catch on roots or joints.
  • •A belly or offset. A low spot or a shifted joint catches solids. A camera finds it.
  • •A collapsed section. If cabling stops at the same spot, a camera shows if the pipe has failed.

Storm drains and the sewer line are different systems

A storm drain carries rainwater away. The sewer carries wastewater. They are separate, and rain should not go into the sewer line. If a yard or patio drain backs up, ask the plumber which system it connects to.

Yard drains and area drains can clog with leaves, soil and debris. They can be cleared with a cable or a jet, and a camera shows where the line runs. Property managers with larger storm drain or catch basin concerns can raise it during the call, and the plumber will say what can be done.

Backflow and why it comes up near the sewer line

Backflow means water flowing the wrong way. A backflow preventer keeps dirty water from moving back into clean water lines. It is a separate device from the sewer, but customers often ask about it during a sewer visit.

A backwater valve on the sewer line itself can stop sewage returning into the house from the main. Whether one is right for your home depends on the fixtures below the street level and the plumber's view of your line. Ask during the visit, and ask the Building & Safety Division at (714) 993-8124 about permits.
