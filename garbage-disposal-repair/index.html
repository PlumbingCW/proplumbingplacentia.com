Safety steps for a leaking disposal or a sink that will not drain

A leaking disposal often shows up as a puddle in the cabinet, or as damp wood that smells after a few days. A sink that fills with gray water means the drain beyond the unit is blocked.

Do these first:

  1. Switch the disposal off at the wall and at the breaker. Never put a hand into the unit or the drain, even when it seems dead.
  2. Stop running water into the sink. Skip the dishwasher too, since its hose empties into the same line.
  3. Put a bucket and towels under the leak. Find where water collects. A drip from the bottom or the side means the unit itself has failed.
  4. Leave drain cleaner alone. It sits in a blocked line and splashes on whoever works on it next.

If several fixtures in the house are slow, the problem is past the disposal. Treat it like the main-line backup described on the drain cleaning page.

What the plumber checks, in order

Disposals look simple, but several things can fail at once. This is the usual order of checks.

  1. 01

    Power and reset

    The plumber checks the switch, the outlet and the reset button on the bottom of the unit.

  2. 02

    Jam and flywheel

    A hex key turned from underneath frees many jams. A seized motor is replaced rather than repaired.

  3. 03

    Seals and mounting

    The sink flange, the gasket and the drain connections are checked for the source of the leak.

  4. 04

    Trap and branch line

    The trap and the line to the wall are checked, because food that passes the grinder lands there.

The dishwasher line, the air gap and other loose ends

The disposal and the dishwasher share a drain, and many calls involve both.

  • ✓The dishwasher drain hose. It connects to a port on the disposal. If the knockout plug was never removed on a new unit, the dishwasher will not drain.
  • ✓A reattached water supply. The plumber can reconnect the dishwasher supply and the disposal drain after a sink or faucet swap.
  • ✓A high loop or air gap. The hose is raised before it joins the line, which prevents dirty water from running back into the dishwasher.
  • ✓A loose mounting ring. The unit hangs from a ring under the sink. A loose ring makes it shake, and the seal leaks.

What not to put down a disposal

A disposal grinds soft food and little else. Grease, coffee grounds, pasta, rice and fibrous vegetables such as celery and corn husks collect in the trap and branch line. Bones and fruit pits can jam the unit.

Run cold water before, during and for a few seconds after grinding. Cold water keeps fat solid so it is carried along rather than coating the pipe. Chemical drain cleaners and hot grease both harm the line, and the installation rules for disposals sit in the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the UPC, and a repeat clog after cleaning is a reason to ask for hydro jetting.
